# How It Works

Five minutes, plain English. Several of these ideas come from two of my
favorite AI creators: Remy Gaskell's free Open Residency episode on AI agents
(https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5p-sq8v3OXw) and Dan Martell's Open Residency
episode, "The AI Cheat Codes Every Founder Needs in 2026"
(https://youtu.be/_24HzGNv-3A). All of it has been run for real in a working
shop.

## Chat answers questions. This does the work.

A regular AI chat answers a question and leaves the work to you. This setup
takes a goal ("get me a follow-up list for this week's quotes"), figures out
the steps, does them, and hands you the finished result to approve.

## Treat it like a sharp new hire who knows nothing about you

Out of the box, the AI is smart but doesn't know your business. It gets good
when you give it three things, the same things you'd give a new office
manager:

| What it needs | What that means | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Context** | Your business, services, prices, customers, how you talk | The `context` folder |
| **Tools** | Access to your email, calendar, job software, CRM | Connectors (see `TOOL-STACK.md`) |
| **Skills** | Your step-by-step processes, written down | The skills folder |

## Your files belong to you

Everything the AI knows about your business is in plain text files in your
own folder, not locked inside one app. If you ever switch AI tools, your
files come with you.

## The files are worth real time

The AI uses your files in everything it writes. If a file says the wrong
price or describes the wrong customer, that mistake shows up everywhere. Read
them. Fix them. Keep them current. The 6-week refresh makes that easy.

## It learns by writing things down, with your OK

The AI doesn't remember conversations. It remembers files. When you correct
it, it suggests where to save the lesson (a price goes in the pricing file, a
rule for estimates goes in the estimate skill) and saves it only when you say
yes. That's how it stops making the same mistake twice without filling up
with junk.

## Skills are your processes

A skill is like the checklist you'd hand a new guy: when to do it, the steps,
what good looks like, what never to do. The best way to make one: do the task
with the AI once until it's right, then say "make this a skill." The first
version won't be perfect. Every time it's off, say "update the skill." After
a few rounds it does it your way.

## Connect your tools, start careful

Connectors let the AI read your email, calendar, jobs, and invoices. Start
every connector on "needs approval" and let it read before it ever drafts or
changes anything. Anything with money stays with you: it can draft the
invoice reminder, you hit send.

## One problem at a time

Every business has one thing holding it back right now: missed calls, emails
nobody answers, quotes nobody chases. The system goes after that one first.
When it's handled, the next one shows up, and you go after that.

## Live in it

The fastest way to get value is to try running a whole workday through it.
It'll be messy the first few days. That's how you find what needs a skill or
a connection. Most people feel the difference within a week.

## The thinking stays with you

It handles the admin. You still decide the price, take the hard call, and
build the relationships.
